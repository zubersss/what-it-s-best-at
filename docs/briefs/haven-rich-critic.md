## The five biggest gaps, ranked

### 1. From 90 to 300 m up the forest is not made of tree crowns.

From 90 to 300 m up the forest is not made of tree crowns. It is flat cut-out stickers with stair-stepped edges. Each has one flat tone, no lit top, no shaded side and no shadow on its neighbours. Many crowns are stretched into diagonal brush smears. In the shaded parts they turn into pale blue-grey see-through discs that look like ghosts. At a low angle from 300 m the canopy is one dark oval stamped over and over, like bubble wrap. The blossoming cherries are the worst of it. From above they are flat tan or beige leaf-shaped scraps and white maple-leaf or snowflake splats with pixel edges, almost none of them pink, and one is a tan capsule lying on the canopy. Because they are the brightest marks in otherwise dark frames, the eye goes to them first, and they read as dead patches or litter.

**Pictures.** 9: green crowns smeared diagonally over the top-left third; tan leaf scraps and white snowflake splats over the left half, e.g. (60,180), (120,285), (225,215), (470,150). 12 (worst case): tan scraps in the upper half; translucent blue-grey ghost discs over the right half (x 450-660, y 100-230). 11: tan paper scraps spread evenly over the canopy, the tan capsule at about (527,277), and the lower half a wallpaper of identical dark discs. 10: grey-white star splats and tan leaf shapes along the bottom edge (x 200-560, y 260-309).

**Why it matters.** The owner's first and strongest request was 'perfect from a distance, specially when I'm flying high above it'. Low flight over the wood is the view a player sees most, and here it looks like a rendering fault rather than a painting. The look asks for trees as bold shapes known by their outline, each crown shaded as one mass, and for trees in bloom, which the owner named. From the air those blooms now read as snowflakes and dead leaves.

**What it would need.** From above every crown should be a solid rounded dome. Its cap on the Sun's side should be a warm green and its far side a cool coloured green crescent, with a short soft shadow falling on the ground or the crowns beside it. Neighbouring crowns should overlap into a lumpy quilt in which each outline can still be traced. They should vary in size and gather into groves with gaps where the ground shows. No crown should be see-through, smeared or flat. A cherry should be a rounded pink dome, pale pink to white on top and rose or mauve on its shaded side, keeping its pink at every distance. A faint pink scatter of fallen petals on the ground under it should drift downwind. Nothing pale should lie flat on the canopy.

### 2. From the air an even blue-teal veil lies over everything, the ground straight below as much as the horizon, so the land looks as if it is under murky water.

From the air an even blue-teal veil lies over everything, the ground straight below as much as the horizon, so the land looks as if it is under murky water. The shade is a heavy navy, close to blue-black, and covers a third to half of each frame. Greens go teal and grey. Distance is not lost in distinct paler layers, because the near ground is already as fogged as the far ground. The painted pair of colours, a warm light and a light coloured shade, is replaced by a warm light and a near-black shade.

**Pictures.** 12: the whole frame under a blue-teal wash, the ground below as veiled as the top edge. 9: a teal murk over the right half and the lower right. 10: the shaded lower right of the wood is close to blue-black. 11: a navy shadow band across the upper third and a dark blue-green hillside. 15: the long valley shadow is a navy-violet wedge that drowns the forest (x 150-660, y 150-309). 13: the forest is a dark teal felt. 16 shows how it should be, with a clear near ground and land paling toward the top.

**Why it matters.** This defines how the world looks every time the player flies, in seven of the eight air pictures, which is exactly the view the owner asked to be perfect. The look calls for coloured shade that is never black and for far ground lost in the air layer by layer. Here the shade is nearly black and the air is one flat fog.

**What it would need.** Looking down from 100 m to 3 km, the ground below should be almost clear. Sunlit forest should be a warm green, sunlit grass gold and rock warm pink. The shade should be a light cool violet-blue at about two thirds of the sunlit brightness, never navy, so that colour can still be read inside it. The air should whiten and cool the land only toward the top of the frame, in two or three clear steps, as it already does at 10 km.

### 3. Seen from the air, the open land is a blank or a blurred map.

Seen from the air, the open land is a blank or a blurred map. Grassland at 30 m is a flat mustard felt with an even fine stipple and no blades, swaths or changes of tone, with stones sprinkled evenly like gravel. At 1 km it is a single ochre colour with soft blotches, and the rocks are tiny dark-blue triangles that look like flies on the lens. The flower meadows leave no trace from above except a cloud of single-pixel orange and dark specks. Sunlit grass slopes are a flat highlighter yellow. Bare high ground from 1 to 10 km looks like soft clay or a stained tablecloth: smooth pink-cream with salmon blotches, no crisp ridgelines, rock faces, gullies or cliff bands, and evenly peppered with single dark and pale pixels. Where forest meets bare ground the edge is an airbrushed blur with a glowing yellow-olive fringe. There is a straight vertical cut through the forest tongue under the plateau. The river between the two lakes is a dashed line of pale beads, like a road, and the lake shores have a pale dotted rim.

**Pictures.** 14: the whole frame one ochre tone, with fly-speck rocks at about (450,175), (520,235), (565,212). 5: an even mustard plane under the horizon, with stones scattered evenly. 9: a speck cloud over the meadow (x 220-500, y 160-230) and lemon-yellow slopes at the lower centre and right. 15: the blotchy pink plateau peppered with specks (x 280-660, y 20-200) and the straight vertical cut at about x 493, y 115-170. 13: specks on the pink strips and a glowing yellow fringe along the middle strip (x 300-520, y 140-200). 16: the beaded river (x 150-230, y 30-110), pale dotted lake rims, and the yellow-green glow over the foothills (x 380-480, y 60-200). 10: a smooth pink, dune-like mountain with pale speckles at the top right.

**Why it matters.** The owner asked for grass that is 'a lot denser in the areas that have it', for flowers abundant in certain biomes, and for rivers and lakes like real ones, all to be perfect from high above. From the air none of it shows: a biome flown over reads as mud or carpet, and the mountains, the main subject at height, read as a blurry map texture. Single-pixel pepper, a seam and a dotted river look like faults, and on a phone they shimmer.

**What it would need.** Grass from above should carry broad painted variation that follows the land: swaths combed by the wind, drier gold on rises, greener hollows, darker damp lines along streams, and sunlit gold paired with a cooler olive shade instead of flat yellow. Flower areas should show from 1 km as soft drifts of tinted colour (blue, white, yellow), with no single-pixel dots. Rocks should either sit in small clusters with a coloured shadow or not be drawn at all where they can only be a speck. Mountains and plateaus should show faceted planes, with lit faces warm, shaded faces a clear coloured shade, sharp ridgelines, gullies and scree fans, and cliff bands at plateau rims. Forest should thin into scattered crowns where it meets bare ground, with no glow, and nothing should change along a straight line. The river should be one continuous ribbon that widens downstream, with a sky sheen and pale banks. Lakes should be lighter and greener in the shallows, and their shores smooth curves with a soft sand or foam edge.

### 4. Up close, the trees still show how they are built: flat cards and sticks instead of foliage carried on a skeleton.

Up close, the trees still show how they are built: flat cards and sticks instead of foliage carried on a skeleton. The cherry at 5 m is an unreadable jumble. It is salmon-pink cut-out sheets in one mid tone, crossing each other at every angle, streaked where they are seen edge-on and holed onto the sky. The branches are straight black rods of even width that float and never fork, taper or join a trunk, so the tree has no shape, outline, lit side or shaded side. Its petals in the air are not legible. The fir's branches are thin straight skewers jutting bare beside the foliage and ending in nothing, while the needle sprays hang as separate flat sheets with straight card edges in front of them. Behind the near trees there are pale mint-to-cyan flat triangles with ruler-straight edges that look like broken geometry. Only the trunks are at reference level.

**Pictures.** 7: the whole frame, with streaked pink sheets, floating black rods across the middle and lower left, and sky holes. 6: bare stubs at the top centre and right, with the sprays as flat sheets beside them, and the pale triangular plane at the lower left (x 20-180, y 140-300). 8: skewer stubs at the top right, pale teal triangular wedges behind the big right-hand trunk (x 750-850, y 120-230), and flat card fronds at the top left.

**Why it matters.** The owner said the trees 'should really be refined: the trunks and the leaves still don't look anything like the references ... specially the leaves and branches', and asked for 'trees with flower petals that bloom'. The cherry is the showpiece of that request and is the weakest picture in the set. The look asks for finely cut foliage on a visible skeleton, each crown shaded as one mass, and here skeleton and foliage are separate pieces.

**What it would need.** Each tree should have a trunk that forks into tapering, curving limbs. Every limb should visibly carry its own clump of foliage or blossom, and no stub should end in air except the odd dead branch. Clumps should be heavy and drooping, darker and deeper in colour toward the inside, with a bright cut rim on top and a cooler coloured underside, so the crown reads as two or three big masses with dark gaps where the limbs show through. No surface should ever be seen edge-on as a streak or show a straight card edge. A cherry should be a few large clouds of pink blossom, deep rose inside and pale where lit, with small distinct petals drifting downwind in loose ribbons and lying on the grass below. Whatever stands behind the near trees should be haze-softened tree shapes, never flat pale triangles.

### 5. Each place has one kind of flower at one size, spread evenly like confetti, and every bloom is a flat sticker turned to face the camera.

Each place has one kind of flower at one size, spread evenly like confetti, and every bloom is a flat sticker turned to face the camera. The opening slope has only a yellow, dandelion-like disc with no stem, cup or side view, all at the same even spacing. The meadow has only one blue cornflower star, at the same density from the feet to the horizon. The steppe has none, and the only second form anywhere is a small lilac spike on the forest floor. There are no drifts, no stretches of bare grass between patches, and nothing standing tall above the grass. The pollen does not read as beautiful: it is tiny white or orange specks spread evenly over the whole sky, high up and above the clouds as well as near the flowers, with only a few soft glows and one blurred disc hanging in the sky. It reads as dust on the lens rather than something the flowers release, and the backlit field has no warm rim on blades or petals. The open grass beneath has the same evenness: a one-tone hatch of thin vertical strokes with no clumps, wind bands or seed heads, unlike the bold two-tone grass of the forest floor.

**Pictures.** 1: identical yellow face-on discs over the whole slope, a blurred disc at the upper left (55,87), and specks over the sky. 2: one blue star kind sprinkled evenly to the horizon, with white specks over the sky and through the cloud. 3: orange dots high in the sky, few of them with a halo, and a flat orange field with no bright rims. 4: no flowers on the steppe and the grass an even olive hatch. 8: the lilac spikes, which show that a second form exists.

**Why it matters.** This is the owner's request almost word for word: 'flowers should be more diverse and abundant in certain areas and biomes, different flowers of different sizes that release pollen into the air that makes it beautiful'. At the moment there is one species per place at one size, with no drifts, and the pollen looks like a flaw in the picture. It is also the first thing seen in the opening view.

**What it would need.** Each flowering place should mix three or four kinds at different heights. Tall spikes such as lupin or foxglove shapes should stand a hand above the grass, daisies and umbels should sit among it, and low carpets of tiny dots should lie under it. Blooms should be heads and cups on visible stems, tilted at many angles, some seen side-on. They should gather into drifts: thick pools of one dominant kind in hollows and along edges, with plain grass between, and each place should have its own bold pair of colours. The steppe should get its own sparse dry-land flowers. Pollen should hang low over the flowering patches, thinning with height and absent from the high sky, and drift in slow loose plumes downwind as soft round motes that bloom into warm halos against a low Sun, with the odd larger seed tuft turning as it drifts. Backlit blades and petals should carry a bright warm rim, so the whole field glows when facing the Sun.
